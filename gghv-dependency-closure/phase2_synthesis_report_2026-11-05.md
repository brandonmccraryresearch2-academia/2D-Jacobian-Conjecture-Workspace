It re-labels Phase 1's three legs, and its layers are not independent: the enumerative layer depends on valuative arguments (Newton polygons), and the eliminative layer depends on the enumerative layer (which pair survives). Dependency arrows run 1b → 2b, 2b → 2c, and 3b → 3a. The arrows license one inference the bare three-leg description does not: they locate the binding constraint — every downstream grade inherits 2b's enumeration conditionality. ## Findings

### SQ1 — Control at infinity

**Verdict: Infinity-control is the real crux only in a narrowed sense — the Jelonek set gives the obstruction a proved shape, and the valuative-tree computation is a hypothesis; the control-theory bridge is a reader-constructed hypothesis, not a literature finding.**

**The Phase 1 half of this verdict (the closure's own record) is taken as given; the literature half is cited below.** Jelonek's theorem makes the obstruction to a Keller map being proper — hence, for Keller maps, to being an automorphism (the properness ⟺ automorphism step not cited from (Jelonek, 1993; Jelonek & Lasoń, 2019) as a single theorem; see 1a(i)) — a set at infinity: $S_f$ is empty or a uniruled hypersurface with an explicit degree bound, witnessed by a rational curve going to infinity (Jelonek, 1993; Jelonek & Lasoń, 2019). The non-trivial content is that *shape* — uniruledness plus a degree bound — and, for generic families, computability from Newton faces (El Hilany, 2019; El Hilany, 2022). What does **not** survive is any suggestion that this is surprising or specific to GGHV: it is the generic situation for polynomial objects, because polynomials are controlled by their highest-degree (at-infinity) parts, and properness is defined by behaviour at infinity. The domains were also chosen because they feature infinity, so the convergence must not be credited as independent confirmation of a crux beyond the Jelonek shape and generic-family computability.

Newton faces compute the obstruction within their scope: for generic (T-BG) families the Jelonek set is computed from Newton-polytope faces alone (El Hilany, 2019), and for generic families (non-)properness depends uniquely on the pair of Newton supports (El Hilany, 2022) — both generic-family scope only, neither transferring to a specific Keller map without extra work; whether the GGHV (72,108) candidate lies in the T-BG family is not addressed anywhere [MATERIAL GAP 10]. In the real case, for the class identified by a Newton-polytope-at-infinity condition on ‖F‖², properness plus det JF ≠ 0 gives global invertibility by Hadamard (Bajbar & Stein, 2018, preprint, real case only) — a sufficient-class condition, not a general control claim, and the determinant condition is load-bearing. For a Keller map, injectivity on one line forces automorphism in dimension 2 (Gwoździewicz, 1993) (the Keller hypothesis Jac ∈ k\* is load-bearing), and homogeneous Keller maps are injective on lines through the origin (de Bondt & Yan, 2013).

The coordinate-free claim splits. **(a) The Jelonek half is proved as a characterisation:** $S_F$ is coordinate-free by definition (Jelonek, 1993). The topological half is a preprint claim: in dimension 2, for a map with nonvanishing Jacobian, non-properness is equivalent to $H_2(N_F) \neq 0$ (Valette & Valette, 2010, preprint). **(b) The valuative-tree half is a hypothesis:** GGHV's (ρ,σ)-directions are *conjectured* points or ends of the canonical tree $V_0$ of valuations centred at infinity (Favre & Jonsson, 2011; Favre & Jonsson, 2004), pending the unwritten dictionary between the valuative tree and Newton polygons at infinity *for a map* [MATERIAL GAP 5]. Favre & Jonsson (2004) is local, and Favre & Jonsson (2011) concerns iteration and degree growth rather than properness of a single map. The qualification is real: P² is the only smooth compactification with irreducible boundary (Mondal, 2013), the Poincaré–Lyapunov variant shows even the compactification's weights are a choice (Dumortier et al., 2006), and dicriticality is relative to the chosen blow-up (Ferrer & Vainsencher, 2010; Cossart & Matusinski, 2011). The literature does not resolve whether a different compactification would make the (72,108) obstruction disappear.

**The control-theory bridge — reader-constructed hypothesis, NOT literature.** The literature supports exactly two solid planks. First, Hadamard properness as above (Bajbar & Stein, 2018) — real not complex, and Pinchuk's counterexample shows det JF ≠ 0 does not imply properness in the real case (Campbell, 1998). Second, Sontag's algebraic observability of polynomial systems (Sontag, 1979): genuinely about polynomials and genuinely about observability — but the system is a discrete-time dynamical system with inputs, the map under study is input/output behaviour rather than a static $F: \mathbb{C}^2 \to \mathbb{C}^2$, and infinity never enters. "Observability at infinity" in control denotes time-infinity (observability at points of the Ω-limit set (Hammouri et al., 2013)) or infinite-frequency/impulsive modes in descriptor systems, never spatial infinity. Control observability is local, Lie-derivative-based distinguishability of states of a dynamical system (Hermann & Krener, 1977; Isidori, 1995; Nijmeijer & van der Schaft, 1990), and in nonlinear control the several definitions of observability are inequivalent (Hespanha et al., 2005) — so any analogy must first say *which* observability it means. The three bridge hypotheses are therefore labelled reader-constructed: (H-B1) properness as observability — bounded output ⟹ bounded state has formally the shape of an output-to-state stability estimate (Hespanha et al., 2005) with the dynamics deleted; (H-B2) dicriticals as unobservable modes — a dicritical is an *anti-invariant* direction (Ferrer & Vainsencher, 2010) against control's invariant distributions (Nijmeijer & van der Schaft, 1990), a metaphor, not a correspondence; (H-B3) the valuative tree as an observability codistribution — an analogy of *roles* (Favre & Jonsson, 2004; Hermann & Krener, 1977), with no functor or theorem connecting them.

**The Jelonek memo formula $\sum_j t_j \cdot \chi(S_j) = d-1$ was NOT found in the literature and remains an unverified hypothesis** [MATERIAL GAP 1]. No source in the corpus states an Euler-characteristic formula for the Jelonek set of that form; Jelonek's published quantitative results concern uniruledness and degree bounds (Jelonek, 1993; Jelonek & Lasoń, 2019).

### SQ4 — "Plane genuinely special" + "selection by extremal/shell structure"

**Verdict: Both remain HYPOTHESIS / UNTESTED CANDIDATE — the plane is proved methodologically special but no dimension-2 mechanism for JC(2) is established, and extremal selection is a real-but-narrow mechanism with two documented failure modes; neither is upgraded.**

**"Plane genuinely special" — evidence for:** the proved asymmetric theorems (tame generation true in 2, false in 3 — the Nagata automorphism is wild (Shestakov & Umirbaev, 2003); all embeddings of the line in the plane are equivalent (Abhyankar & Moh, 1975); Markus–Yamabe true in 2, false in ≥ 3 (van den Essen, 1997)); the method asymmetry (every verification technique is dimension-2-only while the reductions are dimension-free (Bass et al., 1982; de Bondt & Yan, 2013; Guccione, Guccione, & Valqui, 2017; Guccione et al., 2022)); the real/complex divide biting first in dimension 2 (Pinchuk (Campbell, 1998)). **Evidence against / qualifying:** the reductions show the hard core is dimension-free (Bass et al., 1982; de Bondt & Yan, 2013); wildness does not propagate upward (Nagata wild in 3 (Shestakov & Umirbaev, 2003)); Jelonek-set bounds are dimension-free and characteristic-free (Jelonek, 1993; Jelonek & Lasoń, 2019). ## Discussion

**Theme 1 — Infinity is the invariant object; coordinates are the bookkeeping — narrowed.** The convergence holds only in the narrowed sense of SQ1 (Jelonek shape proved; generic-family and real-case computability; valuative-tree half a hypothesis; topological half a preprint claim with its hypothesis restored). The bookkeeping *is* choice-dependent: P² is the only smooth compactification with irreducible boundary (Mondal, 2013), weights are a choice (Dumortier et al., 2006), and dicriticality is relative to the chosen blow-up (Ferrer & Vainsencher, 2010; Cossart & Matusinski, 2011). *Alternative:* the theme may be an artefact of corpus selection — the domains were chosen because they feature infinity, the RQ primes infinity, and properness is defined at infinity — so GGHV's real invariant may be finite combinatorial data, with infinity where that data is *read*, not what it *is*.

**Theme 2 — Dimension 2 is methodologically special; no mechanism is proved.** The asymmetric theorems are as stated in SQ4 (tame generation in 2 only; line embeddings equivalent in the plane; Markus–Yamabe in 2 only; the real conjecture already failing in 2), while Bass–Connell–Wright reduce JC to cubic homogeneous maps in *any* dimension by stabilisation (Bass et al., 1982), sharpened to Drużkowski maps (de Bondt & Yan, 2013): the difficulty is dimension-free; the tools are not, and Nagata is wild in 3 (Shestakov & Umirbaev, 2003). *Alternative:* the specialness is an artefact of the search, which looked for dimension-2 tools; a corpus assembled around the reductions, Jelonek bounds, and the Alpöge counterexample would conclude dimension 2 is the *residue* left after every dimension-free method fails, not a positive specialness.

**Theme 3 — Generic is the wrong neighbourhood — with the inference labelled.** Jouanolou's theorem — a very generic foliation of degree ≥ 2 has no invariant algebraic curve (Jouanolou, 1979) (read secondary only) — and Ferrer–Vainsencher's count — dicriticality is a positive-codimension condition (Ferrer & Vainsencher, 2010) — together imply, *on the reading of a Keller map's fibres as leaves of its associated foliation (an identification no source in the corpus states)*, that GGHV's objects sit in the complement of generic behaviour. El Hilany's theorems are proved for generic families (El Hilany, 2022; El Hilany, 2019) and do not transfer to a specific Keller map; whether the (72,108) candidate lies in the T-BG family is unaddressed [MATERIAL GAP 10]. *Alternative:* this states a triviality — a Keller map has constant Jacobian, a positive-codimension condition by definition, so *of course* generic theorems do not apply, and GGHV never invoked them; the relevant contrast is specific-vs-specific (this candidate vs other Keller maps), for which the corpus has nothing.

**Theme 4 — Extremal facts are real; extremal *selection* is not established.** The Alexander–McTague and D₄ cases are as stated in SQ4: the shell-counting argument correctly identifies the favoured *bifurcation* (bcc) and even the critical droplet near the spinodal, yet the predicted stable phase is wrong (fcc wins) and every AM-satisfying crystal is unstable (Alexander & McTague, 1978; Groh & Mulder, 1999; Klein, 2001); D₄ is a strict local minimum of Epstein zeta for every s > 0 (Sarnak & Strömbergsson, 2006) yet is *not* universally optimal as a spherical code (Cohn et al., 2007), and universal optimality is proved only in dimensions 8 and 24 (Cohn et al., 2022) (dimension 1 being the trivial case). Once variational structure is broken, the Maxwell point has "no bearing" on outcomes (Raja et al., 2023); far from equilibrium there is in general no extremal principle at all (Cross & Hohenberg, 1993). *Alternative:* the AM/D₄ analogies are a category error — pattern and lattice selection are dynamical/thermodynamic, while the closure's extremal facts are static enumeration facts — so the theme should be reframed as "no selection mechanism is even statable here," not "selection is real-but-narrow elsewhere."

**Theme 5 — Elimination certifies consequences, not provenance.** The elimination facts are as stated in VEE 3a/3b: the last elimination ideal is always principal, so the Gröbner result's *form* is routine and its *content* — the single generator being exactly the predicted target — is substantive (Gallet et al., 2015; Cox et al., 2015), and the leg's three-route agreement is a meaningful triangulation of the *computation* (Sturmfels, 2002; Gallet et al., 2015). But the Closure Theorem gives only the Zariski closure of the projection, and extension/lifting can fail (Cox et al., 2015); the leg established "IF the 9 equations hold THEN the target holds" and left the derivation of the 9 equations open — a step elimination theory cannot certify (Cox et al., 2015; Greuel & Pfister, 2008; Gallet et al., 2015). *Alternative:* the conditional boundary is an artefact of the formalisation style — every Lean result is conditional on its hypotheses by construction, and every Singular computation is conditional on its input ideal — so the binding constraint is not "conditionality" in general but the four specific imports in SQ3, and Theme 5 adds nothing beyond SQ3.

**Implications.** The closure is a chain of *conditional* results, with the enumeration premise at the top, whose computations are strong and whose conditions are imported, and the imports — not the computations — are now the binding constraint. **Reflexive limit.** This premise-ranking is conditional on the Phase 1 record as given and on the table's completeness: it ranks the imports the closure actually carries, and if that record or that table is wrong, the ranking moves with it. The evidence that would distinguish "the imports are binding" from "conditionality is a formalisation-style artefact" is an import failing in a way that changes a *mathematical* outcome — for example, the enumeration missing a survivor, or Joseph Lemma 2.2 proving false in the homogeneous form used — versus an import merely adding a hypothesis label with no downstream mathematical change. Until such evidence appears, "imports are the binding constraint" describes this project's evidence as recorded, and the Theme 5 alternative stands unrefuted alongside it. The assumption that would alter the problem is the one SQ3 makes visible: that the imported premises hold in the form used. Alter the enumeration premise and the 125 ceiling itself moves; alter Joseph Lemma 2.2 or Lema Joseph and the central assembly collapses; obtain the published §7 text and the hybrid-vs-slip question, and possibly the m,n > 1 gap, is settled. No other assumption survives scrutiny as one the closure actually made: the genericity and P²-stability clauses are annexed as reader-constructed framing (H6), not findings.

**Devil's-advocate counter-arguments and how they were handled.** The strongest counter-argument is that the draft's load-bearing move — GGHV's machinery as a *computation of coordinate-free invariants* — was drawn across a dictionary the draft itself listed as unwritten, from a corpus assembled by searching for infinity, with the control-theory leg admitted to be reader-constructed. It was handled by splitting the claim (M3): the Jelonek half stands as a proved characterisation and the topological half as a preprint claim, the valuative-tree half is moved to the Hypothesis Annex, and the convergence rhetoric is dropped in favour of the deflation (M2). Against each SQ verdict: SQ1 survives only in the narrow Jelonek-shape sense; SQ2 is downgraded to untested, not refuted (M8); SQ3's "one boundary" is replaced by three failure modes with the enumeration premise as a chain; SQ4's theses may be a search artefact and a category error respectively; SQ5's ceiling depends on a preprint enumeration and a carried completeness claim. The VEE counter-argument — that the framework re-labels Phase 1's three legs, whose layers are not independent — is handled by stating plainly that VEE is an organisational scheme, not a literature finding, and by showing the dependency arrows (1b → 2b, 2b → 2c, 3b → 3a). ## Conclusion & Recommendations

**What Phase 1 points to.** The closure points at infinity, but only in the narrowed Jelonek-shape sense; at 125, because the enumeration stops there; and at the imported premises, because they are now the binding constraint. On the two recall-leg theses, the plane is proved methodologically special with no dimension-2 mechanism established, and extremal selection remains an untested candidate whose documented failure modes do not transfer to a static algebraic enumeration. For readers outside this project: cite 125 as GGHV's enumeration ceiling, conditional on [GGV5] completeness — never as an established bound.

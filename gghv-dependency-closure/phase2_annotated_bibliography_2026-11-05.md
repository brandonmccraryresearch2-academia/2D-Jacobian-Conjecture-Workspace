## Tier 1 — Peer-reviewed journal articles and monographs (42)

**V1. Abhyankar, S. S., & Moh, T. T. (1975). Embeddings of the line in the plane. *Journal für die reine und angewandte Mathematik*, *276*, 148–166.**
WHY: the classical dimension-2 unknotting theorem, a pillar of the "plane genuinely special" thesis. HOW: proves every embedding of the affine line in the plane is equivalent to the standard one, via degree-divisibility arguments. WHAT: all line embeddings in the plane are equivalent, while embeddings in A³ are not. Relevance: SQ1 (direction/valuation flavour), SQ4 (methodological specialness). Weakness (reader-inferred): the degree-divisibility mechanism is never connected to JC(2) in the corpus. Read scope: abstract only. Tier 1. Verification note: corpus page range corrected 149–166 → 148–166 (IAS repository).

**V2. Alexander, S., & McTague, J. (1978). Should all crystals be bcc? Landau theory of solidification and crystal nucleation. *Physical Review Letters*, *41*(10), 702–705. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.41.702**
WHY: origin of the extremal-selection pattern (shell counting predicts the stable phase). HOW: Landau theory of solidification; counts ordered zero-sum triads on the reciprocal shell. WHAT: predicts bcc as the stable phase for weakly first-order freezing. Relevance: SQ4 (selection thesis; the ● marks the claim's existence, not its correctness). Weakness (reader-inferred): contested by V20/V30 — the predicted stable phase is wrong. Read scope: secondary (Klein 2001 §II summary + citing-paper abstracts); not read directly. Tier 1.

**V3. Arnold, E. A. (2003). Modular algorithms for computing Gröbner bases. *Journal of Symbolic Computation*, *35*(4), 403–419. https://doi.org/10.1016/S0747-7171(02)00140-2**
WHY: computational background of the 125 frontier (cited as SuperMind manuscript ref [1]). HOW: modular algorithms for Gröbner bases. WHAT: faster exact elimination machinery of the kind the certificate legs rely on. Relevance: SQ5 (background), SQ2/SQ3 (T4, partial). Weakness (reader-inferred): no JC content; background only. Read scope: cited-in-manuscript only; full text not opened. Tier 1.

**V4. Bass, H., Connell, E. H., & Wright, D. (1982). The Jacobian conjecture: Reduction of degree and formal expansion of the inverse. *Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society, New Series*, *7*(2), 287–330. https://doi.org/10.1090/S0273-0979-1982-15032-7**
WHY: the dimension-free reduction programme — the main counterweight to dimension-2 specialness. HOW: stabilisation reduces JC to cubic homogeneous maps in any dimension. WHAT: the hard core of the conjecture is dimension-free. Relevance: SQ1, SQ4, SQ5. Weakness (reader-inferred): none noted; the reduction is proved but does not itself advance JC(2). Read scope: abstract + sections. Tier 1.

**V5. Becker, T., & Weispfenning, V. (1993). *Gröbner bases: A computational approach to commutative algebra* (Graduate Texts in Mathematics, Vol. 141). Springer. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-0913-3**
WHY: textbook foundation for the elimination leg (Gröbner bases, elimination ideals). HOW: graduate text, computational commutative algebra. WHAT: the general elimination theory the Singular leg's principality claim rests on. Relevance: SQ2, SQ3 (T4). Weakness (reader-inferred): textbook; no direct JC content. Read scope: contents. Tier 1. Verification note: Kredel is "in cooperation with", not a co-author on the title page.

**V6. Brazovskii, S. A., Dzyaloshinskii, I. E., & Muratov, A. R. (1987). Theory of weak crystallization. *Zhurnal Eksperimental'noi i Teoreticheskoi Fiziki*, *93*, 1110–1124. [English: *Soviet Physics JETP*, *66*, 625–634 (1988).]**
WHY: fluctuations correct mean-field extremal predictions — a failure mode for selection claims. HOW: theory of weak crystallisation with fluctuation corrections. WHAT: fluctuations admit phases (other cubic phases, 1D density wave, possibly icosahedral states) that mean-field theory forbids. Relevance: SQ4 (T3, partial). Weakness (reader-inferred): Russian original; English translation cited. Read scope: abstract only + introduction excerpts. Tier 1.

**V7. Cohn, H., Conway, J. H., Elkies, N. D., & Kumar, A. (2007). The D₄ root system is not universally optimal. *Experimental Mathematics*, *16*(3), 313–320. https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0607447**
WHY: the D₄ non-optimality result — the second documented failure mode for extremal selection. HOW: constructs a better spherical code than D₄. WHAT: D₄ is not universally optimal as a spherical code. Relevance: SQ4 (T3, diverges). Weakness (reader-inferred): proves nothing about lattice packing; the D₄ optimality criterion differs from IRH's S-criterion. Read scope: sections. Tier 1.

**V8. Cohn, H., & Kumar, A. (2007). Universally optimal distribution of points on spheres. *Journal of the American Mathematical Society*, *20*(1), 99–148. https://doi.org/10.1090/S0894-0347-06-00546-7**
WHY: sets the standard a selection claim must meet. HOW: proves universal optimality via bounds for completely monotonic potentials. WHAT: minimisation for *all* completely monotonic potentials, proved by a bound — the standard a single extremal ratio does not meet. Relevance: SQ4 (T3). Weakness (reader-inferred): sphere problem, not lattice problem; scope differs from D₄/E₈ claims. Read scope: abstract only. Tier 1.

**V9. Cohn, H., Kumar, A., Miller, S. D., Radchenko, D., & Viazovska, M. (2022). Universal optimality of the E₈ and Leech lattices and interpolation formulas. *Annals of Mathematics*, *196*(3), 983–1082.**
WHY: universal optimality proved in dimensions 8 and 24 — the positive narrow case for selection. HOW: interpolation formulas proving universal optimality of E₈ and the Leech lattice. WHAT: universal optimality in 8 and 24 (dimension 1 being the trivial case); the 2D triangular lattice remains conjectural; dimension 3 is "a zoo". Relevance: SQ4 (T3). Weakness (reader-inferred): E₈/Leech optimality does not transfer to D₄. Read scope: sections. Tier 1.

**V10. Cossart, V., & Matusinski, M. (2011). Dicritical divisors after S. S. Abhyankar and I. Luengo. *Journal of Algebra*, *342*(1), 147–153. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2011.05.032**
WHY: dicriticality is relative to the chosen blow-up — a bookkeeping caveat for SQ1. HOW: theory of dicritical divisors. WHAT: dicriticality is a property of the chosen resolution, not of the map alone. Relevance: SQ1, SQ4. Weakness (reader-inferred): none noted. Read scope: abstract + sections. Tier 1.

**V11. Cox, D. A., Little, J., & O'Shea, D. (2015). *Ideals, varieties, and algorithms: An introduction to computational algebraic geometry and commutative algebra* (4th ed.). Springer. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-16721-3**
WHY: textbook source for the elimination boundary (Closure Theorem, extension/lifting). HOW: graduate text. WHAT: the last elimination ideal is always principal; the Closure Theorem gives only the Zariski closure of the projection; elimination certifies consequences of a given ideal, not its provenance. Relevance: SQ2, SQ3 (T4). Weakness (reader-inferred): textbook; no direct JC content. Read scope: contents. Tier 1.

**V12. Cross, M. C., & Hohenberg, P. C. (1993). Pattern formation outside of equilibrium. *Reviews of Modern Physics*, *65*(3), 851–1112. https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.65.851**
WHY: review canon for pattern formation; far from equilibrium there is in general no extremal principle. HOW: review article. WHAT: in variational pattern formation near onset the selected pattern minimises a known functional; far from equilibrium no extremal principle holds in general. Relevance: SQ4 (T3, partial). Weakness (reader-inferred): review article; pattern-formation canon, not a JC source. Read scope: abstract only. Tier 1.

**V13. de Bondt, M., & Yan, D. (2013). Some remarks on the Jacobian conjecture and Drużkowski mappings. *Journal of Algebra*, *380*, 244–257. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgebra.2013.03.015**
WHY: sharpens the dimension-free reduction and gives the lines-through-the-origin injectivity result. HOW: Drużkowski maps; homogeneous Keller maps injective on lines through the origin. WHAT: the hard core is dimension-free; low-cost directional hypotheses suffice for homogeneous Keller maps. Relevance: SQ1, SQ4. Weakness (reader-inferred): none noted. **V14. Dumortier, F., Llibre, J., & Artés, J. C. (2006). *Qualitative theory of planar differential systems* (Universitext). Springer. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-540-32902-2**

**V15. El Hilany, B. (2022). Counting isolated points outside the image of a polynomial map. *Advances in Geometry*, *22*(3), 355–374. (arXiv:1909.08339v3)**
